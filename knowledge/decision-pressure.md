# Session: Decision-Making Under Pressure

Core business question: How can people make high-quality decisions when time is limited and information is incomplete?

Competitive chess forces repeated decisions under a clock. Players rarely have enough time to calculate every possibility. Strong decision-making therefore requires a process for deciding what deserves calculation and what can be handled with pattern recognition or principles.

Themes include time pressure, incomplete information, confidence calibration, sunk cost, deciding what deserves deeper analysis, avoiding impulsive action, recognizing when a decision is reversible, and separating process quality from outcome quality.

A good decision can still produce a bad outcome. A bad decision can occasionally produce a good outcome. Teams should therefore review the quality of the reasoning process, not only the final result.
